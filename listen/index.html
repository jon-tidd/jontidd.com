    What your taste says about you

    This is a read, not a diagnosis: patterns in the genres, artists and habits below, interpreted the way a friend with your listening history open might.

    Retro-future escapist

    Gunship, Perturbator, Carpenter Brut and Oscillian are all top-tier, and synthwave is the one genre that shows up in all 12 years. That reads like someone who likes the future as the 80s imagined it: chrome, night highways and a soundtrack to the movie in your head.

    Builds over hooks

    Toundra, Long Distance Calling and the 7-minute “Epic Build” list suggest a patience for songs that earn their payoff. Post-rock and instrumental music is often what people reach for when they want momentum without lyrics competing for attention.

    Heavy, but warm

    Melodeath (Amorphis, Eluveitie, In Flames) and gloom (Katatonia) sit next to Puscifer’s dry humor. That mix often belongs to listeners who use heavy music for release and catharsis, not anger.

    Loyal core, restless edges

    The same dozen artists hold steady while hundreds of new ones arrive every year. One reading: you know what home sounds like, and you still keep the door open.

    Desert-road wanderer

    The 2023–2026 swing to stoner and desert rock (Slomosa, Nightstalker, King Buffalo) adds fuzz, open space and campfire grit to the mix.

    Album, not algorithm

    Shuffle is on for only about 12% of plays, and most plays start because the previous track ended. That pattern usually points to someone who respects the record as the artist sequenced it.

    The kind of people who tend to share this mix

    • Builders and makers who code or design to instrumental, wordless momentum.
    • Sci-fi and 80s-cinema fans who came to synthwave through movie and game soundtracks.
    • Ex-metalheads with grown-up jobs who never stopped loving the riff.
    • Road-trippers and campers for whom desert rock and post-rock are the scenery soundtrack.

    A generalization drawn from the genres, not a claim about any specific person.
